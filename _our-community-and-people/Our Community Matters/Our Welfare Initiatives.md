<h2>Journeying with Low-Income Families</h2>
<p>Queenstown is home to 13 rental blocks, each filled with families working
hard to make ends meet. Some juggle multiple jobs, others do their best
while quietly struggling to put food on the table. Food is a basic need.
Yet, not everyone finds it easy to ask for help. Some may not know where
to go, who to turn to, or even what support is available.</p>
<p>Together with volunteers, food charities, and community partners, we’ve
been building a more inclusive and supportive Queenstown, one where<strong> </strong>help
is not just available, but accessible, and where residents are supported
with care, empathy, and dignity.</p>
<h3>Food from the Heart Community Shop @ Lengkok Bahru</h3>
<p>Opened in February 2022, the Community Shop @ Lengkok Bahru is a collaboration
with Food from the Heart to bring targeted food support to Queenstown families.
Operating three days a week - Tuesdays, Thursdays, and Saturdays, the shop
lets beneficiaries pick 14 essential items each month, such as rice, canned
food, and biscuits. It’s a dignified, shop-for-free concept that allows
families to choose what they need, when they need it. To keep it running,
Queenstown has also held multiple donation drives, including a division-wide
drive in March 2025 that raised nearly 14,000 food items worth $48,000.</p>
<h3>Happy Mart @ Stirling by Hao Ren Hao Shi</h3>
<p>Happy Mart, launched in October 2024 at Block 49 Stirling, is a community-run
store that brings food support closer to where residents live. Open every
Wednesday and Sunday, it operates on a credit system where families receive
12 monthly credits to select food items they need. The shop not only provides
practical aid but also encourages dignity and independence in food choices,
much like the Community Shop at Lengkok Bahru.</p>
<h3>Mobile Grocery Truck by Hao Ren Hao Shi</h3>
<p>To reach residents who face mobility or transport challenges, Hao Ren
Hao Shi also runs a mobile grocery and essentials truck. Since July 2023,
the truck has been making monthly stops at Block 3 Jalan Bukit Merah on
the second Saturday of every month. Eligible families receive up to 20
essential food items, delivered right to their doorstep. With over 150
applications to date, this initiative continues to meet a growing community
need.</p>
<h3>Project 100 = 50</h3>
<p>Project 100 = 50 is a pop-up supermarket concept that allows low-income
families to stretch their dollars by paying $50, each household brings
home up to $100 worth of groceries and essentials. Held every second Saturday
at the open basketball court at Leng Kee CC, this monthly programme is
supported by Sheng Siong, Food from the Heart, and Standard Chartered Bank.
Since its introduction in Queenstown in 2021, it has supported 300 families
and continues to offer meaningful, dignified assistance.</p>
<h3>FoodConnect Credits</h3>
<p>To provide flexible, dignified food support for families with children
under 21 living in rental housing, we launched the FoodConnect Credits
initiative in partnership with GovTech, MSF, and SSO. Piloted in 2023 and
since expanded, beneficiaries receive monthly food credits through the
LifeSG app, which can be used via SGQR codes at participating supermarkets,
hawker centres, and local merchants. This system empowers families to decide
what foods best meet their nutritional and personal needs while maintaining
convenience and choice.</p>
<h3>Community Fridges @ Stirling View</h3>
<p>The Community Fridges initiative, active since 2018, has helped improve
food accessibility and promote community care in Queenstown. A notable
partnership with Fridge Restock Community SG at Stirling View has helped
spotlight food sustainability by encouraging the use of “ugly” but edible
produce and reducing waste. With over 200 beneficiaries, the project continues
to be supported by local RCs and leaders who are working with businesses
and volunteers to keep the initiative fresh and relevant.</p>
<h3>Community Fridge by Mdm Marlina</h3>
<p>When Mdm Marlina noticed some neighbours skipping meals or worrying about
groceries, she took the initiative to start a community fridge along her
corridor. Roday, it continues to serve residents in need, quietly and meaningfully,
with dignity.</p>
<h3>Mendaki Tuition Programme</h3>
<p>Launched in 2012, the free tuition programme at Leng Kee CC, in partnership
with Mendaki, provides academic support to primary-level students (P1–P6)
of all races. This initiative aims to improve educational outcomes by delivering
high-quality tuition, fostering a supportive learning environment, and
promoting inclusivity. Held in the afternoons, the programme offers free
access to educational resources and personalised tutoring, helping students
enhance their academic performance and build a strong foundation for future
learning. Promotion of this initiative is carried out through Mendaki’s
website and posters at Leng Kee CC. Over the past 12 years, the programme
has supported around 200 students.</p>
<h3>Free Chinese Physician Clinic @ Queenstown CC</h3>
<p>Since 2018, Queenstown has offered free traditional medical consultations
through a partnership with The Singapore Buddhist Lodge. Held every Tuesday
from 10am to 4pm at Queenstown CC, this initiative provides free consultations
and low-cost medication for residents in need. Promoted via posters and
word of mouth at Queenstown and Leng Kee CCs, the service has reached approximately
250 residents over the past six years, ensuring accessible, community-based
healthcare.</p>
<h3>Free Haircut for Seniors @ Leng Kee CC</h3>
<p>Launched on 5 May 2024, this initiative - led by our community partner
Oriental Radio - offers complimentary haircuts to seniors in Queenstown.
Every first Sunday of the month, skilled volunteer barbers provide their
services at Leng Kee Community Club, helping seniors stay well-groomed
with ease and dignity. To date, around 60 seniors have benefitted from
this initiative.</p>
